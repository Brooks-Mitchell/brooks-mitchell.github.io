AEC Environment Fundamentals

Brooks Mitchell · Version 1.0 · October 2026

From site acquisition to closeout, and the software and AI that run it.

Most people in AEC know one part of a project well. An architect knows design but has never read a pro forma. A superintendent knows the jobsite but was never in the room when the land was bought. A developer can underwrite a deal but has never written an RFI.

The people building software for this industry often know even less, because they learned the one workflow their product serves.

This course follows a building from the first look at a site through design, construction, and turnover. At each step it covers who does the work, what documents they produce, what software they use, and where AI is changing the job.

Who this is for

Someone with real experience in one part of the built environment who wants a working model of the rest of it. An architect who has never seen a capital stack. A founder selling into construction who needs to know what happens before the drawings exist. An engineer who wants to understand why the budget was never negotiable.

It assumes you are competent in your own lane and have no obligation to know anyone else's.

How it's organized

Three parts that follow the life of a project in order, plus a synthesis module that runs one project through all of it.

Part 1 — Real Estate Development

How sites are analyzed and bought. A deal through five decision gates, from the first look at a market to closing on the land and the financing.

  1. The Development Ecosystem and Deal Lifecycle
  2. Market and Site Selection: Is There Demand?
  3. Legal and Regulatory Due Diligence: What Are You Allowed to Build?
  4. Physical Due Diligence: Vacant Land / Existing Buildings and Infrastructure
  5. Concept Feasibility and Cost: Does It Physically Pencil?
  6. The Capital Stack: Whose Money and What Does It Demand?
  7. Site Control and the Transaction
  8. The Handoff to Design

Part 2 — Design and Construction

One reference project — a 200-unit garden-style apartment community — from the day the developer closes on the land to the day the property manager receives the keys.

  1. The Handoff: From Pro Forma to Project

  3. Cost Through Design: Estimating and the Budget Gap
  4. Reading the Document Set
  5. Buying the Work: Procurement, Contracting, and Buyout
  6. Preconstruction Planning: Schedule, Logistics, Permits, and Risk
  7. Building It: Field Execution and Contract Administration
  8. Closeout, Turnover, and Handback to Operations

Synthesis — Lifecycle Recap

One project through all three parts in sixteen steps. For each step: who is involved, what they produce, which tools they use, what AI does today, and what information gets lost in the handoff to the next step.

Why I wrote it

I started my post-college career as a Field Manager in residential construction in Orlando, driving site to site, coordinating trades for windows, doors, trusses, trim, and stairs. In between then and now I did software engineering for oil and gas accounting, and IoT software and hardware. Today I help architects and real estate folks implement AI into their workflows.

I had a smattering of knowledge from construction, architecture, and operations, and wanted a complete and thorough understanding of how everything fit together and flowed. So I wrote this for myself. I drew on my own experience and hundreds of hours of conversations with AEC professionals of all kinds, and I used LLMs in the writing.

I learned an immeasurable amount writing it, and I hope you gain just as much from reading it. Feedback, questions, and criticisms are welcome — reach out on LinkedIn.
